About Me

I am currently a senior majoring in Computer Science and minoring in Artificial Intelligence at Cornell University, where I am fortunate to be advised by Claire Cardie. I have also collaborated with Thorsten Joachims and Qi Yang on interactive and human-centered AI, including LLM-based academic peer review and multimodal creativity support.

My research interests lie broadly in natural language processing, machine learning, and reinforcement learning, with a focus on advancing the generalization and adaptability of modern AI systems. I am particularly interested in developing AI systems with reliable reasoning and autonomy, enabling continual self-improvement through interaction, and extending models beyond a single modality to support human-centered applications in real-world domains.

Research

Behavioral Evaluation and Control in AI-Simulated Patients

Summer 2025 – Present

Advisor: Claire Cardie, Collaborator: Yann Hicke

  • Investigating behavioral consistency in large language models used as AI-simulated patients for medical education.
  • Developing methods to detect and control these behaviors using supervised classifiers, multi-agent systems, and reinforcement learning, enabling more realistic and pedagogically aligned simulations.

OmniWizz — Multimodal Inspirational Tool

Summer 2025 – Present

Advisors: Qi Yang and Ge Guo

  • Addressed the need for a tool to inspire musical creativity by enabling seamless transformation across text, image, and audio, fostering cross-modal idea generation and exploration.
  • Designed and implemented the full-stack architecture for a unified multimodal framework, delivering flexible input–output combinations to empower diverse and innovative creative workflows. [View Demo]

Automatic Synthetic Data Generation

Spring 2025

Advisor: Claire Cardie

  • Exploring automated dataset construction as a step toward self-evolving models, focusing on scalable synthetic data generation for continual domain adaptation in medicine.
  • Built a hierarchical knowledge map expansion pipeline that recursively generates 226 subfields and produces 2.9K+ expert–student dialogues and MCQA items without human annotation.
  • Fine-tuned a compact student model, achieving +6.76% accuracy over baseline while using 6.4× fewer training samples than supervised methods.

Culturally and Temporally Contextualized Lyric Generation

Spring 2025

Advisor: Matthew Wilkens

  • Investigating how to adapt large language models for lyric generation that is culturally, temporally, and emotionally grounded.
  • Curated a 31,000-song corpus and fine-tuned LLaMA 3.1 8B with LoRA for context-aware generation.
  • Achieved +67.4% ROUGE-2 and +18.9% human ratings over baseline systems.

Automatic Code Generation System

Fall 2024

Advisor: Claire Cardie

  • Tackled the challenge of enabling LLM-based code generation to autonomously complete complex, multi-step software engineering tasks.
  • Built a multi-agent framework with automated task discovery, curriculum learning, progress monitoring, and rollback recovery, and evaluated it on the Commit-0 benchmark.

Enhancing Multi-Hop Reasoning in LLMs

Summer 2024

Advisor: Claire Cardie

  • Tackled LLM hallucinations and reasoning errors in multi-hop QA.
  • Developed Reasoning Court, extending ReAct with two retrieval–reasoning agents and an LLM judge capable of selecting or synthesizing factually grounded, coherent answers.
  • Outperformed few-shot prompting baselines on HotpotQA, FEVER, and MuSiQue, achieving up to +7.2% EM and +10.5% F1 improvements while reducing LLM calls compared to hybrid baselines.

Computer Music Synthesis and Composition

Summer 2023

Advisor: Roger B. Dannenberg

  • Explored mapping the digits of π to the minor pentatonic scale to algorithmically compose music, simulating traditional Chinese instruments and producing an original countermelody.

Algorithms for Big Data

Summer 2021

Advisor: David Woodruff

  • Addressed the high computational cost of GAN-based cartoonization for resource-constrained devices by developing LWAnimeGAN, reduced channel sizes, and removal of HourGlass blocks, reducing parameters by 8.8%, FLOPs by 56.1%, and inference time by 18.6% while preserving visual quality.
